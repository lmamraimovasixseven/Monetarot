Policy-rate corridor and carry trade

A scenario tool, not an exchange-rate forecast. Real rate = nominal rate − year-on-year inflation; a partner's monthly rate is the average over the calendar days of the month. Now (2026-09): NBK rate 16.25%, inflation 9.30%, real rate 6.95%. Partner real rates: Russia: 7.65% (month 2026-09; real differential KZ − Russia -0.70 pp); China: 2.10% (month 2026-09; real differential KZ − China +4.85 pp).

China's rate: China policy rate (BIS; LPR 1Y / benchmark lending rate). It is a lending benchmark, not the return on a short-term deposit, so the real rate and the corridor for China rest on a lending rate (D47).

Corridor bounds: 18.95–22.95% with the default risk premium and cap (upper bound set by the cap on the real differential); the NBK rate of 16.25% is below the corridor: risk of outflows into the carry trade.

Credit constraint: slope of credit growth 6 months ahead on the real rate 0.168 (the slope is not negative, so the credit constraint is not identified and is not used).

Regression of the monthly change of 100·log USD/KZT (monthly mean of the official NBK rate) on the real differential KZ − the higher of Russia and China, months 2015-09–2026-09: differential at t−1: coefficient +0.286 (HC1 s.e. 0.130, p = 0.028, R² = 0.049, n = 131); with t−1 and t−2: coefficient on t−1 +0.512 (p = 0.220), VIF 4.98. Verdict: the relation is significant at 5% (HC1). The sign is positive: a higher differential is followed by a rise in USD/KZT (a weaker tenge), the opposite of what carry-trade inflows would imply. This is an in-sample association, not a causal estimate and not a forecast; omitted variables such as the oil price, global risk appetite and the rouble can bias it.

Benchmark: the exchange rate as a random walk (forecast change = 0); mean absolute monthly change of 100·log USD/KZT 1.878. A three-country panel with fixed effects is an illustration only: three units do not give reliable standard errors. It is built when the data are available.

The same regression on the differential to each partner separately:

  • KZ − Russia: coefficient on the differential at t−1 +0.285 (HC1 s.e. 0.103, p = 0.006, R² = 0.096, n = 131); the relation is significant at 5% (HC1).
  • KZ − China: coefficient on the differential at t−1 +0.092 (HC1 s.e. 0.078, p = 0.242, R² = 0.008, n = 131); no relation is visible (p ≥ 0.05, HC1).
