Evidence

Holdout comparison, falsification, diagnostics, robustness, stress tests and history of the model. The numbers come from the dashboard block of the artifact; the model is not re-estimated here.

Ordered logit meets the pre-specified criterion against Always Hold on the holdout (RMSE ratio 0.995, below 1). This does not show that the model forecasts better. Robustness results are mixed.

The 95% block-bootstrap interval for the RMSE ratio (0.780 to 1.335) contains 1, and the Diebold–Mariano test (HLN-corrected) gives p = 0.961, so on 18 holdout decisions the model cannot be distinguished from Always Hold in either direction.

Effect of the data update (OLD = git tag before-data-update-2026-09-07, NEW = current files): usable decisions 88 → 89; estimation sample 70 → 71 decisions (ending 2024-06-03 → 2024-07-15); holdout 18 → 18 decisions (2024-07-15–2026-09-07 → 2024-09-02–2026-10-27). The holdout RMSE ratio changed from 1.002 to 0.995, so the pre-specified rule's label changed from "does not meet the pre-specified criterion (RMSE ratio not below 1)" to "meets the pre-specified criterion (RMSE ratio below 1)". 95% bootstrap intervals: OLD 0.828 to 1.230, NEW 0.780 to 1.335; both ratios lie inside both intervals, so the change does not show that the model forecasts better or worse than before.

Always Hold assigns probability zero to Increase and Decrease, so the log score cannot be computed on a holdout that contains them.

Ordered logit meets the pre-specified criterion against Always Hold on the holdout (RMSE ratio 0.995, below 1). This does not show that the model forecasts better.

Supplementary measure (exploratory, added after the holdout had been seen; the pre-specified verdict stays RMSE-based): the ranked probability score on the same holdout (lower is better) is 0.154 for the model, 0.194 for Always Hold and 0.159 for the class frequencies of the estimation sample. Simple class frequencies do almost as well as the model, so the six factors add little beyond the base rates of cuts, holds and hikes; most of the gain over Always Hold comes from admitting that cuts and hikes happen.

Limitations

Real release lags are UNKNOWN. The model assumes data for month M−2 are available at a decision in month M, which leaves about 45 days (median 44; 31 to 59 across decisions); `outputs/release_lag_check.md` uses the stricter shortest window minus one day (30 days). File update dates give only upper bounds on the release lag (true lag ≤ bound): inflation 1 day; exports 66 days; imports 66 days; corporate lending 24 days; household lending 24 days; consumption 37 days. For exports the update date belongs to the edition ending 2026-07; the later rows up to 2026-08 come from a newer edition whose publication date is UNKNOWN. For imports the update date belongs to the edition ending 2026-07; the later rows up to 2026-08 come from a newer edition whose publication date is UNKNOWN. Look-ahead cannot be excluded for 89 of 89 decisions for exports and imports (and for 15 of 89 decisions for consumption). The nearest existing check is RB07 ("Ordered logit: release lag 3 months", which shifts all factors): benchmark winner Model beats Hold, survives No; a check that shifts only the trade series was not run. Revision status of all series is UNKNOWN.

In RB09 (Differenced non-stationary factors), household lending has coefficient 0.3651 (raw p 0.201, Holm p 0.452), against Holm p 0.047 in the main model; the only significant factor loses significance when its non-stationarity is removed.

Falsification criterion 3 (holdout RMSE ratio ≥ 1) is not met: on the 18 holdout decisions the RMSE ratio is 0.995, below 1; the difference from 1 is too small to say the model is better; we can only say it is not worse.

No time-stamped Week-4 pre-registration was submitted. The instructor allowed the model specification (docs/model_spec.md) to be accepted with the date of its commit, 16 October 2026 (commit f763e16). Exploratory backtests were run before the specification was committed; see docs/deviations.md D15.

Interpretation of the data update: the holdout is the last 18 usable decisions (2024-09-02 to 2026-10-27); appending a decision moves the unchanged chronological split rule, so the estimation and holdout windows shift with the data. 1 of these decisions (2026-10-27) took place after the specification was dated (2026-10-16); the other 17 had already been seen in earlier runs. The holdout is therefore partly newer data but still not an untouched confirmatory holdout.

The probabilities shown for the next NBK decision are the model's probabilities at the latest decision in the data (2026-10-27), computed from its factor month 2026-08 (decision month minus 2). They are not recomputed for the meeting of 2026-12-04 itself, which under the same rule would need factor month 2026-10.

Exploratory: added on 2026-11-06 after the holdout had been seen. The pre-specified main model and its verdict are unchanged; holdout numbers here are non-confirmatory.
  • Headline RMSE ratio 0.995. The 95% block-bootstrap interval for the RMSE ratio (0.780 to 1.335) contains 1, and the Diebold–Mariano test (HLN-corrected) gives p = 0.961, so on 18 holdout decisions the model cannot be distinguished from Always Hold in either direction.
  • Supplementary measure (exploratory, added after the holdout had been seen; the pre-specified verdict stays RMSE-based): the ranked probability score on the same holdout (lower is better) is 0.154 for the model, 0.194 for Always Hold and 0.159 for the class frequencies of the estimation sample.
