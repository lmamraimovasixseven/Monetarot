# Related work

Three sources added after the freeze (D53). Each description says only what was checked in the source on 2026-11-17. The five references of the technical note are in `docs/references.md`; this list does not replace them.

- **Kim, T.-H., Mizen, P., & Thanaset, A. (2006).** Forecasting Changes in UK Interest Rates. Granger Centre Discussion Paper No. 06/06, University of Nottingham, November 2006. https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/research/groups/grangercentre/documents/06-06.pdf. A dynamic ordered probit with three outcomes (increase, decrease, no change) for the Bank of England's rate decisions. Because "no change" dominates, the share of correct forecasts can mislead, so the paper uses Merton's measure (CP), on which a "stopped-clock" strategy scores zero. Out-of-sample forecasts use an expanding window. Checked in the full text (abstract, sections 3 and 6).
- **Zholdas, S., & Sharif, A. (2023).** Central bank text shocks, monetary surprises, and inflation expectations in Kazakhstan. Master's thesis, Nazarbayev University Graduate School of Business. https://nur.nu.edu.kz/handle/123456789/8174. Uses NBK press releases; the residuals of a Romer–Romer-style regression are treated as monetary policy shocks. English-language press releases have no significant effect on market expectations, Russian-language ones do. Checked in the abstract on the repository page; the full text is gated and was not read.
- **National Bank of Kazakhstan.** Statement – base rate (decision of September 2026). Statement by the Governor, Astana, 4 September 2026, posted on the BIS website (date delivered 22 September 2026). https://www.bis.org/speeches/20260922-statement-base-rate-national-bank-kazakhstan. Read in full; the factors it names are compared with the model on the Methods and limits page (`config/nbk_statement_drivers.json`).
